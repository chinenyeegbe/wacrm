# Pre-visit brief for a Moldlane sales conversation

Research a local service business from **public sources only** and
produce a one-page brief the founder can skim in two minutes before
walking in. The goal is to arrive already knowing their reputation,
their repeat-service angle, and the pitch math — like a well-prepared
peer, not a cold seller.

## Sources (public only — never scrape private data)

- Their website and any booking page (services, prices if listed,
  service-plan offers)
- Google Business Profile: rating, review count, review *velocity*
  (how recent), and what reviewers praise or complain about
- Checkatrade / Trustpilot / Which? Trusted Traders listings if present
- Companies House (UK): incorporation date, size signals, directors
- Facebook/Instagram business pages: posting activity, WhatsApp links
- Anything indicating they already use WhatsApp with customers
  (wa.me links, "WhatsApp us" buttons, number on the van/website)

If web access is unavailable, produce the template with the user's
notes and mark the gaps to check on arrival.

## Output format

```
## <Business name> — pre-visit brief

**Snapshot:** trade, location, years trading, team-size estimate,
review profile (rating × count × recency).

**Repeat-revenue angle:** the specific recurring clock in THIS trade
(MOT reminders, annual boiler service, spring/autumn cleans, service
contracts) and any evidence they do or don't work it today.

**Reputation notes:** 2–3 themes from reviews (what customers praise —
these become message copy later; any complaint pattern — never mention
it in the pitch, but avoid promising against it).

**WhatsApp readiness:** evidence they already use WhatsApp with
customers, or which number to propose connecting.

**Pitch math (say this out loud):**
"You've got roughly <N> past customers. Most businesses like yours
have <~60% × N> they haven't heard from in six months. If we message
them and even <10–15%> book — at your average job of <£X> — that's
<£Y> of work you already earned once. Moldlane sends those messages
for you, from your own WhatsApp number."

**Three talking points:** specific to this business (reference a real
review, their service plan, or their trade's calendar).

**One question to open with:** something they'll want to answer
(e.g. "How do you remind people their MOT is due?").

**Things NOT to say:** no jargon (CRM, automation platform, API), no
promises about new-customer lead gen — Moldlane recovers existing
customers.
```

Keep the whole brief under a page. Numbers beat adjectives.
